Room 01 · Heat

The sea remembers
every degree.

Sea surface temperatures across the Pacific have warmed beyond the range of natural variability. The anomaly that was once a brief statistical outlier is now the baseline. Since 2000, the ocean has not returned to its pre-industrial memory.

Room 02 · Rain

The sky has lost
its rhythm.

Rainfall across Pacific Island nations swings between drought and deluge with increasing ferocity. The monsoon patterns that sustained cultures for millennia are becoming erratic, unpredictable, extreme. Every bar below the line is a drought. Every bar above is a flood.

Room 03 · Rising

Land is becoming
a borrowed thing.

Pacific sea levels have risen steadily since satellite measurement began in 1993. For island nations with average elevations under two metres, this is not an abstract statistic — it is the loss of home. Tuvalu. Kiribati. Marshall Islands. Each centimetre is irreplaceable.

Room 04 · Loss

Numbers behind
the silence.

Cyclones, floods, droughts — the disasters are intensifying. In 2016, Cyclone Winston — the most powerful tropical cyclone ever recorded in the Southern Hemisphere — displaced over 630,000 people in Fiji alone. The losses compound across generations.

Room 05 · Future

Resilience is
being written now.

Pacific nations are not passive observers of their own fate. Access to safe water grows steadily. Solar energy is transforming island power grids. The memory of what was lost is becoming the blueprint for what must be built.
